What I learned

Describe your design: hypervisors, tenants, VNIs and addresses. Which tenants overlap, and why did you place each VM where you did?

Four hypervisors share the 10.40.0.0/24 underlay. Amber uses VNI 494001 and violet uses VNI 494002. Both use 10.40.60.0/24, so am1 and vi1 both have .10, while am2 and vi2 both have .11. Amber runs on hv1, hv2 and hv3; violet runs on hv1, hv3 and hv4. This gives each tenant three VTEPs to test flooding, gives hv1 and hv3 both tenants, and makes hv3 an observer of amber traffic between hv1 and hv2. The shared subnet is intentional: the VNI, rather than an IP allocation rule, provides isolation.

Two of your VMs have the same IP address. Using your evidence, explain how a ping reaches the right one — and what would have to go wrong for one tenant to see another's traffic.

am1's three pings to 10.40.60.11 reached am2 on hv2, even though vi2 on hv3 owns the same address. Amber's ARP and ICMP stayed on br494001 and vxlan494001; vi2's simultaneous capture recorded zero packets. The neighbor entry on am1 identifies am2 by its 02:07:89:b1:00:0b MAC; vi1's entry for the same IP identifies vi2 by 02:07:89:b2:00:0b. The different VNI bytes in those MACs help inspect the result, but the actual boundary is separate bridges and VNIs. Joining a violet VM port to the amber bridge, or mapping traffic to the wrong VNI at a VTEP, would break that boundary.

Using your evidence, explain what was flooded, to which hypervisors, and what each VTEP learned. With 50 hypervisors in one VNI, how many copies would one broadcast need — and how would EVPN change that?

On a fresh build, hv1's remote VTEP FDB has only all-zero flood entries pointing at amber's other VTEPs, hv2 (10.40.0.2) and hv3 (10.40.0.3); the bridge also shows its own permanent local MAC. am1's first ARP request was copied to both. hv3 captured the request with VNI 494001 even though the target am2 was on hv2. Afterward, hv1 learned am2's 02:07:89:b1:00:0b at 10.40.0.2 and hv3 learned am1's 02:07:89:b1:00:0a at 10.40.0.1. Known-unicast packets then need only one remote VTEP. With 50 hypervisors in a VNI, one head-end replicated broadcast needs 49 underlay copies. EVPN advertises MAC/IP locations through BGP and can answer ARP at the VTEP, avoiding many discovery floods; genuine broadcasts still need distribution.

Compare your two MTU fixes using your measurements. Which would you choose for a data centre, what does each cost, and who has to change what?

The forced 1500-on-1500 design passed small pings but lost both 1472-byte pings and transferred zero bytes at the TCP receiver in this run. The DF boundary was payload 1422 working and 1423 failing. Fix A limits each tenant to 1450 bytes, exactly 1500 minus VXLAN's 50-byte overhead. A 1422-byte ping payload fills that 1450-byte inner IP packet, whereas 1423 is too large at the tenant interface. Fix B keeps the tenant MTU at 1500 and raises the underlay to 9000, letting a 1472-byte payload fit but rejecting 1473 at the VM. The measured five-second receiver rates were 3.62 Gbit/s for A and 3.89 Gbit/s for B on this virtual runner; one run is too little to claim a reliable performance difference. I choose fix A for this four-hypervisor demonstration because it needs only the VM-side MTU policy and works on standard 1500-byte paths. It costs 50 bytes of tenant packet size and can reduce effective TCP throughput. A real data centre with end-to-end jumbo support could choose B to keep standard 1500-byte tenant packets, but every underlay interface and any transit path must consistently support the larger frames; a single 1500-byte segment would recreate the black hole.

If your hypervisors' VTEPs were P4 switches running the lecture's vtep.p4, what would have to fill the vtep_encap table, and how? What would replace flood-and-learn?

The P4 control plane would install vtep_encap entries keyed by the tenant-facing ingress port and destination MAC. Each action supplies amber's or violet's VNI, this switch's VTEP address, the remote VTEP address, the next-hop MAC and the underlay output port. It would also fill vtep_decap entries keyed by VNI and inner destination MAC for local delivery. The lecture's vtep.p4 deliberately has no ARP or flooding, so these mappings must be supplied at run time. With EVPN, BGP advertisements of each VM's MAC/IP location would populate them before traffic; alternatively a controller could receive source-learning digests and write the entries, since the P4 data plane cannot add table entries itself.
