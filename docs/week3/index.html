What I learned

Describe your design: why these routers, LANs and links, and how you chose the addresses.

I put r1, r2, r3 and r4 in a ring, with one host LAN on each router. There are two physical directions between any pair of routers. I kept every network inside my 10.40.0.0/16 block: the LANs are 10.40.1.0/24, 10.40.16.0/24, 10.40.32.0/24 and 10.40.33.0/24, while the four router links use separate /30 networks. I chose adjacent 32 and 33 LANs so r1 can describe both with one summary route.

Which route summarises several LANs? Which networks does it cover, why is it correct, and what else would it attract if you added a network inside its range?

On r1, 10.40.32.0/23 via 10.40.41.1 summarises the h3 and h4 LANs, 10.40.32.0/24 and 10.40.33.0/24. Both are reached through r4 from r1; r4 forwards h3 traffic onward to r3. The summary matches any address from 10.40.32.0 through 10.40.33.255, including a future subnet carved from that space, even if that subnet belongs somewhere else. Those two current /24 LANs already fill the /23, so adding a separate subnet there would first require renumbering or subdividing one of them.

When you took a link down, what happened, and why couldn't your routes recover even though another path existed? What would have recovered?

With the r1-r4 link down, h1's trace to h4 timed out, and r1's route lookup reported Network is unreachable. Linux removed the static route through the down interface. The ring still offered r1-r2-r3-r4, but no static alternate route had been installed. After bringing the link back up, I reapplied the affected routes to restore reachability. A tracked backup route or a dynamic routing protocol such as OSPF could choose the surviving path automatically.

In your network, what was the control plane? Who worked out the routes, and what does that mean for a network of 40 routers?

I acted as the control plane by choosing the routes in network.yaml; check_paths() checked the intended paths, and build_routed.py installed them. Each Linux router then forwarded packets using its own routing table. Doing this by hand on 40 routers would mean many next hops to calculate and keep consistent after each change, so a routing protocol or automated controller would be more practical.
